# NabungFi problem evidence for the future pitch

Prepared on 9 October 2026. This is the approved research input and draft problem sequence for a future deck, not an exported presentation or evidence of NabungFi's behavioral impact. Public copy and narration are in English.

## Problem beat: protecting the intention

**Slide title:** A savings goal needs more than a balance.

**Visible copy:**

- Pooled funds blur what each deposit is for.
- Easy withdrawals leave the commitment easy to reverse.
- Small deposits need a visible connection to the goal.

**Visual direction:** One hypothetical saver choosing a car goal, with a pooled balance, a withdrawal action and a small deposit. Use a clearly illustrative scene, not a fabricated user testimonial. Keep the three frictions readable; do not depict numeric balances as inherently ineffective.

**Draft narration:**

> Imagine saving for a car. Each deposit starts with that intention, but the money still sits beside everything else you can spend. You can withdraw it before the goal is reached, and a small change in the balance may feel distant from the car itself. We wanted to connect the money, the commitment and the progress to one clear goal.

## Evidence beat: mechanisms worth testing

**Slide title:** Separation and commitment have helped savers follow through.

**Visible copy:**

| Research result | Context that must remain visible |
| --- | --- |
| Approximately 72% more saved | Two cash envelopes versus one; rural India |
| 82% higher average savings balances after one year | Group offered a commitment account versus comparison; Philippines, J-PAL summary |

**Source line:** Soman & Cheema (2011); Ashraf, Karlan & Yin (2006), summarized by J-PAL. External studies; not NabungFi outcomes.

**Visual direction:** Two native editable evidence cards with study-specific labels and separate amounts/contexts. Do not add the percentages together or use a single chart that implies the studies share a population or measurement.

**Draft narration:**

> There is evidence behind these mechanisms. In one Indian field study, dividing earmarked cash between two envelopes led to about seventy-two percent more saved than one envelope. In the Philippines, offering a commitment account increased average savings balances after one year. These are results from other settings. They motivate our design, while NabungFi's own impact still needs testing.

## Bridge into the solution

**Draft narration:**

> NabungFi gives a chosen goal its own USDC vaults and withdrawal rules. Its model takes shape as funded progress grows. Separation and commitment inform the financial design; the build experience is our hypothesis for making progress easier to see.

Show the actual goal-local flow next. Preserve the current testnet scope wherever otherwise the audience could infer a production product handling real savings. Do not claim mainnet safety, realized yield or proven habit formation.

## Sources and claim boundaries

| Source | Verified finding used | Boundary for slides and speaker notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Soman & Cheema (2011), Earmarking and Partitioning](https://www.russellsage.org/sites/all/files/u137/jmr-C-s014-s021-online.pdf) | 146 workers; 14-week total savings means of 414 rupees in two envelopes versus 241 in one. The relative difference is (414 / 241 - 1) x 100 = 71.8%, rounded to 72%. Photo conditions averaged 350 versus 304 rupees. | Cash-envelope setting. Not proof that multiple digital goals or 3D models increase saving by the same amount. Photos were reminders, not a test of construction animation. |
| [J-PAL, Commitment Savings Products in the Philippines](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/commitment-savings-products-philippines?lang=en) | Summary reports an 82% increase relative to the comparison group after one year. Withdrawals restricted to a chosen amount or date; ordinary and commitment accounts paid the same interest rate. | Use the summary's 82% consistently. This is the group offered the product, not an effect estimated only among account openers. Take-up was 28.4%; locking was not universally chosen. |
| [Ashraf, Karlan & Yin (2006), Tying Odysseus to the Mast](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/121/2/635/1884028) | Original randomized evaluation; 1,777 baseline clients, 710 offered SEED, 202 opened accounts. | The paper abstract uses different reporting wording: "81 percentage points." Do not silently relabel it as the J-PAL 82% figure or cite the original alone for that exact headline. |
| [Cheema & Bagchi (2011), The Effect of Goal Visualization on Goal Pursuit](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jm.75.2.109) | Easier visualization supported effort and commitment near the goal. | No numerical effect for NabungFi. Do not claim numbers-only interfaces cause failure, or 3D is superior to a conventional progress bar. |
| [Soman & Zhao (2011), The Fewer the Better](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmr.10.0250) | A single goal could outperform several competing goals under certain conditions. | Partitioning money does not mean more simultaneous goals always help. A primary-goal UX is a design implication, not an already-tested NabungFi feature. |

## Product validation to plan

Test a numeric/progress-bar experience against the build experience while keeping goal rules and funding steps constant. Define repeat confirmed deposits and goal completion as outcomes; engagement with animation alone is not saving success. Record withdrawals or unmet liquidity needs when evaluating commitment, rather than treating a larger locked balance alone as improved financial health. Testnet usage can establish usability, but cannot by itself establish real-money saving behavior.

## Claims to avoid

- "NabungFi increases savings by 72% or 82%."
- "Most people fail because their balance is only a number."
- "More goals always mean better saving."
- "Locking savings helps everyone."
- "The 3D model is scientifically proven to form saving habits."

The full deck's order and timing remain to be adapted to the submission brief. Carry these contexts into both visible source labels and speaker notes when the deck is produced.
