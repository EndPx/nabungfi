# Problem

Saving for something specific often starts with a clear intention and becomes harder to track as deposits spread across wallets and networks. A wallet balance shows available money, but it does not explain which part belongs to a car, a laptop or another commitment. Multiple addresses and network-specific balances add another layer of bookkeeping.

## A wallet does not enforce a goal

A name in a spreadsheet or a mental reservation does not prevent spending. Someone who wants a stronger commitment needs rules attached to the funds themselves. Those rules also need to remain understandable: what is locked, what counts toward the target, and what action actually makes the funds available?

## A portfolio total can hide a shortfall

Suppose a car has 80 USDC saved and a laptop has 20 USDC saved. A portfolio total of 100 USDC does not mean the laptop’s 100-USDC target is reached. Treating shared totals as goal eligibility would weaken the original commitment and let one goal borrow another goal’s reserves.

## Cross-chain balances need a common decision

Even within one goal, a visible total is only an observation. One selected chain might still be waiting for a message or a verified reserve. Users need a clear distinction between reaching the target, verifying completion and receiving funds through a claim.

## Progress should feel tangible

A number alone can make repeated small deposits feel disconnected from the intended outcome. NabungFi explores whether building a recognizable object can communicate progress more clearly. This is a product hypothesis to validate with users, not a claim of measured improvement in savings behavior.
